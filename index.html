Need a hand? Get help with Sagito

Big dreams. Small steps. A little adventure every day.

Sagito is 11+ practice that a child actually wants to open. Every question answered moves an expedition forward — there are wildlife cards to find, coins to earn and a camp that becomes their own. The practice is real and the reward is theirs.

Coming to Google Play soon.

Forty wildlife cards, illustrated by Sagito

Every card is an original illustration of British wildlife, and each one has an evolved form to find as an explorer goes on. They are earned by practising — never bought, and never behind a countdown.

And a camp to spend the coins on

Everything in the camp is drawn for Sagito too, from the tent to the lantern. Coins come from practice alone, so a camp is a record of work done rather than money spent.

Four subjects, 5,000 questions

  • Mathematics — number, fractions, geometry, data and word problems
  • English — comprehension, grammar, spelling, vocabulary and sentences
  • Verbal reasoning — codes, letter patterns, analogies, synonyms and hidden words
  • Non-verbal reasoning — matrices, series, odd one out, reflection, rotation and hole punch

2,000 of the 5,000 questions are free, permanently and with no daily cap — around six months of daily practice before anything is asked of you.

It finds the right level on its own

Sagito works out where a child is from their first set of answers, and moves with them question by question. An explorer who is finding it hard meets warm-ups; one who is flying gets stretched. There is no level to choose and nothing to configure, and the difficulty never caps how far anyone can go.

Practice never stops

No daily limit, no energy meter, no countdown. When every free question has been seen, Sagito keeps going and serves the ones met least often first. An explorer is never stopped mid-session and asked to buy something.

Shaped for particular schools

Some children are working towards one school in particular. School focus has sets in the style of five of them — Queen Elizabeth’s (Barnet), St Olave’s, Wilson’s, Henrietta Barnett and Tiffin Girls’ — so a child on one of those paths can practise the kinds of question that school has set before. Every question in School focus is Stretch level or harder.

Every explorer deserves a companion

A friendly companion offers encouragement, tells a few jokes and shares fun facts — bringing a smile to practice time and helping children feel supported as they learn. The adventure is better shared, too: children can swap cards and gift items with friends and family, as a request the other can decline rather than something that happens to them.

More than one explorer

Each explorer keeps their own progress, cards and camp, and each can have a PIN so a brother or sister cannot answer in their place.

For the grown-ups

A PIN-protected area shows how each explorer is doing by subject and topic, which areas need work, and how much practice they have done. Purchases live behind that PIN and nowhere else. Nothing is ever sold to a child.

There is no Sagito server

No account, no sign-in, no advertising and no analytics. Nothing your child does is collected, transmitted or seen by us, because there is nowhere for it to go — everything stays on the device. The only network conversation the app has is with Apple or Google about a purchase, and it carries nothing about your child. Read the privacy notice.

What costs money

Everything above is free. Two optional purchases sit in the grown-up area, behind the parent PIN and nowhere else:

  • Extra camps, £2.99 once — four new places to pitch camp, with two items for each. Decoration only; they give no advantage in any question or score.
  • Sagito Plus, £3.99 a month or £39.99 a year — every question in the main bank of 5,000, every school set in full, and the four extra camps included.

Neither is needed, and nothing an explorer has earned is taken away if a subscription ends.

What a subscription pays for

Sagito is built and maintained in the UK, and it carries no adverts and no analytics — nothing about your child is collected or sold, so there is no income behind it other than these two purchases. Keeping 2,000 questions free, permanently and with no daily cap, is a deliberate choice: we would rather as many children as possible could practise than put the whole bank behind a paywall.

That is what a subscription supports. It pays for questions to be written, reviewed and corrected, for new wildlife cards and camp items to be illustrated, for the bank to keep pace with the 11+ as it changes, and for the work of keeping the app running well on the phones and tablets families actually own. Every update reaches everyone, free and paid alike. If Sagito is useful to your explorer, subscribing is what keeps it being made — and keeps it open to families who cannot.

Kept accurate

Questions are reviewed and updated as and when it is needed — to correct anything found to be wrong, and to keep pace with changes to the 11+ curriculum and how it is examined. Updates reach everyone, free and paid alike.
