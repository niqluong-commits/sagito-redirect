The short version

Some extra campsite decorations can be bought if you want them; they change how the camp looks and nothing else. 6. Buying things

The app is free to use, and practice never stops. There is no daily limit, no energy meter and no countdown. When every free question has been seen, Sagito keeps going and serves the ones met least often first. A child is never stopped mid-session and asked to buy something.

Two things can be bought, and both live in the grown-up area behind the PIN.

  • Extra camps, a one-off purchase. Four more places to pitch camp, with two items each. These are decorative: they change what your camp looks like and give no advantage in any question, test or score.
  • Sagito Plus, a subscription, billed monthly or yearly — whichever you choose when you subscribe. Two thousand questions are free permanently; Plus opens the rest of the bank, and includes the camps. We add questions from time to time and Plus covers those too — but we do not promise any particular number, or any schedule, and nothing here should be read as one. Either billing period renews until you cancel, and you cancel it in the store account you bought it with.

Keeping the questions right. We review and update the question bank as and when it is needed: to correct anything found to be inaccurate, and to keep pace with changes to the 11+ curriculum and the way it is examined, so that a student is practising against current material rather than something out of date. Updates reach every version of the app, free and paid alike — accuracy is not something we would put behind a payment.

This is a commitment to keeping the questions right, not to a timetable. There is no set period between updates, none is promised, and a stretch without one does not mean anything is wrong. Equally, it is not a guarantee about any particular examination: 11+ papers vary by school and by region, they change without notice, and we are not affiliated with any exam board or school.

8. Friends, gifts and swaps

Children can connect to friends by QR code or link, and then send each other cards and gifts. Both ends of this sit behind the parent PIN, so an adult approves every connection on both devices.

There is a second route, added on 15 September 2026, which makes no connection at all: a card given to a friend in the same room. The giving phone shows a QR code and the friend's phone scans it off the screen. The two households never become connected, and no adult approves the other family — so unlike everything above, a child can accept a card from someone no grown-up has met.

What limits it is the code rather than an approval: it lasts two minutes and is only accepted from a camera, so it cannot usefully be forwarded or photographed, and it carries the giving child's display name and which card and nothing else. Nothing is stored about the other child and there is no way for either to reach the other afterwards.

A grown-up can switch this route off, under Swap cards in person in the grown-up area, behind the PIN. It is on until they do.

There is no chat: no messages, no comments and no free text passes between users. The only thing one child's device shows another is the display name they chose and the card involved.

Two things we ask of the adult:

  • Only approve people you actually know. A code carries no adult's name and no location, and both ends are PIN-gated, but the judgement of who to connect to is yours and we cannot make it for you.
  • Choose the display name with that in mind. It is shown to the other family. A nickname is fine and we suggest one.
